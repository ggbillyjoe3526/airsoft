# Features

What the game has on `main` today, by area: one line per feature, with the ids that added it. The changelog agent keeps
this file (`.claude/agents/changelog.md`).

Depot is playable from the Match screen against four stand-in figures (FP1). The browser game's list,
`legacy/browser/docs/FEATURES.md`, is the parity list: each line is a feature to rebuild, change on the owner's word,
or drop (`docs/ROADMAP.md` › What 0.1 is).

## Simulation rules

- BB flight: BBs fly with drag, gravity and hop-up lift; the AEG Rifle stays on target to about 38 m, the GBB Pistol to about 29 m at 20 °C and about 26 m on Woodland at night (FP1)
- Hit calling: one hit eliminates; you raise an arm, call "HIT!" and walk off to the dead zone round cover, where you watch through a teammate's eyes; bots call their hits honestly (FP1, BE1)
- Loadouts: up to five named loadouts, one carried into each match; each replica has its own BB weight (0.20 to 0.40 g), hop-up dial, colour and power source, with batteries setting the AEG Rifle's rate of fire and gas the GBB Pistol's power, capped at the field's chrono limit; bots keep their replicas as they come (LO1)
- Inspect replica: Y (rebindable in Settings › Controls) turns your replica side on, its muzzle to the left, and looks it over while its parts move: an AEG's charging handle draws back, its dust cover opens and the hop-up dial turns; a GBB Pistol turns over with its slide home; a Cyber Pistol's battery slides out of the grip and back; about 3 s for rifles, 2 s for pistols; any shot, aim, reload, switch, holster, sprint or hit stops it at once (IN1)
- Replica colours: your replica wears the colours picked in your loadout, in your hands; bots hold theirs (Alpha in Cobalt and Onyx, Beta in Signal and Coral), and the Cyber Pistol keeps its own; Realistic colours shows bold schemes as plain colours from the next match, and turns the Cyber Pistol plain grey with no glow (LO3, CP1)
- Replica wear: the AEG Rifle and GBB Pistol look lightly used, with faint scratches, rubbed edges, dust in the recesses and bare metal only where rubbed hard, following the look's wear setting, their swappable magazines too; bots, the Cyber Pistol and the Sniper Rifle stay clean (RW1, RW2)
- Holster: press 3 to holster the replica (out of view, "Holstered" panel, no crosshair); holstered you run faster than with it in hand, never faster than a sprint, and sprint a tenth faster; a trigger pull, aim, reload, slot key or mouse wheel draws it again, slower than a normal switch; bots holster on long calm runs (MO1)
- Elimination rounds: first to 5 against the clock, with ends swapped at half time; a finished match rematches 15 s after its summary shows, or on a click (FP1, SM1)
- Attack / Defend flag rules: attackers raise the flag at the defenders' pole in 7 s and then hold it up for 3 s to win (a defender on the rope pulls it down and the hold starts again), defenders pull it down in 3 s, and both teams at the pole hold it still; a time-out wins for the defenders, with up to 15 s of overtime while attackers are on the rope or a raised flag is held (FG1, BP8)
- Overtime flag in Elimination: when the clock runs out with both teams in, a flag goes up at the map's spot; the first team to touch it wins the round, two teams at it hold each other off, a wipe-out still wins, and 30 s untouched is a draw played again; bots react by level, run for it (some round a flank), anchors and careful bots watch it from cover first, and each team's nearest bot calls it; the chequered flag rises at the spot with a reach ring, the clock shows OT and the flag's time, a banner and three short whistle blasts mark it, the marker and minimap show it, and a round won there ends FLAG TOUCHED (OV1, OV2, OV3)
- Attack / Defend in play: pick it on the Match screen (Depot or Woodland, maps with a flagpole), with a flagpole and reach ring at the defenders' flag spot, the flag climbing in the attackers' colour, a FLAG gauge with 12 notches, ATK and DEF tags, OT on the clock, the "Their flag is going up!" warning, a "FLAG · 24 m" marker, round banners and XP and records as Elimination (FG3); Depot has two poles, A and B, either raised wins, each lettered, named on the marker and the warning, both on the minimap (AB1)
- Replicas: AEG Rifle (semi, burst, auto), GBB Pistol (semi) and Cyber Pistol (semi, burst, auto; level 15; built-in battery, CP1), magazines swapped on reload, the GBB Pistol's slide locks back on empty until the reload seats a magazine (MZ1), a draw on switching, light recoil and spread that grows when moving or jumping and tightens when crouching, holding still or aiming down sights, read from `data/stats.md`; shots, dry clicks and magazines play recorded sounds (FP1, AU2)
- Compact SMG (in development, Dev content): a compact electric SMG for close quarters, 18 BBs a second, quicker on the move with half the spread, shorter reach at about 30 m (0.85 J), semi and full auto, level 10 (CS1, LV15)
- Start mode per map: on Depot the AEG Rifle and Cyber Pistol, and bots' replicas, start each round on single shots, and bots switch to auto only now and then at someone in open space down a long sight line; switching to auto mid-round still works; other maps are unchanged, and later indoor maps can opt in (NA1)
- Replica parts: red dots, scopes, grips, hi-cap and low-cap magazines, barrels, silencers and the laser (the pistol's, and the AEG's on its side rail) change how a replica plays; parts are picked on the Customise screen and show on the replica in your hands; the two barrels have models, the tight bore's inner barrel in its hop-up unit and the long barrel's extension (AT1, AT2, AT3, BA1)
- Wind: on open fields only (Woodland and the Range), a light breeze of 0.3 to 1.8 m/s, new every match; it pushes BBs, never players, and dust specks drift with it; Depot and Subway are still air; each field has its own air temperature, which gas replicas feel (BB1)
- Hop-up dial: Page Down (less) and Page Up (more) turn each replica's dial for the match; the replica panel shows the setting and flat-shot range; bots keep factory dials (BB1, MC1)
- Ricochets: BBs bounce off concrete and steel containers; by default a bounced hit doesn't count ("Ricochet: play on"), and a match rule can make it count, not yet in a menu (BB1)
- Movement: walk, run, sprint, crouch, a limited jump (FP1), lean with Q or E (slows to a walk; eases back when a wall cuts it short) and vault over low walls with Jump, with "Space to vault" under the crosshair, and climb up onto a platform edge with Jump, with "Space to climb" under the crosshair; bots climb platforms too and lean in on stairs going up, sitting back going down (CL1); bushes and ferns slow everyone to three quarters, and running through them rustles louder and further (BE1, MO1, BP8)
- HUD: the pale bone accent marks highlights, such as your own lines in the hit feed, where yellow was; top bar with each team's score as pips toward the win, round clock, round number and FIRST TO 5; slanted banners for round start, half time with ends swapped, and who took the round or match; bottom right replica panel with BB count, fire-mode chips (S, B, A) with the current one lit, a box per magazine (loaded first, then fullest spare), HOLSTERED, RELOADING, LAST MAG or OUT OF BBS, and a low magazine turns yellow; a weapon rail above the ammo panel with a picture and key per replica and an icon per bomb carried, the one in hand lit (BP8); a 3, 2, 1, GO! countdown holds every round's start, beeping on 3, 2 and 1 and with the referee's whistle at GO (BP8); a crosshair that opens with your spread (FP1, MZ1, HD1)
- Hit feed and teammate markers: a hit feed in the top right shows who fired, the BB trail and who was hit with its HIT call, and your own hit stays framed in it until the next round; teammates wear a chevron that shows through walls and greys out when hit (HF1, WG1)
- What got you card: when your hit is called, a card on the left shows the shooter's side, distance, held angle, time in view and whether you were moving, when known; the HUD tab's What got you setting is Auto (Hard bots only), On or Off (WG1, HO1, TL1)
- Skipping the round: once out for 1.5 s (clicks before then don't count, so the fight's own clicks never skip), click (Fire) to skip; the rest is played out at once, unseen, behind a "Skipping the round" screen with no fast-forward or sound, then the round's result shows and the next round waits for a click (after the last round, the match's results follow); the pause menu's Skip to next round does the same and shows while you can skip; with Settings › Gameplay › Simulate skipped rounds on (off by default), a SIMULATE button (a click, never Fire) decides the round at once by a seeded roll weighted by players left and flag progress, marked Simulated (WG1, MC1, UF2, BP8)
- Round result and table: a banner says WON, LOST or DRAW from your side at each round's end, then the round's table slides in with each team's score, every player's hits, BBs fired, time in play and who hit them; after a skip the next round waits for a click, and a round played to the end moves on by itself (RR1, MC1)
- Scoreboard on Tab: hold Tab for the compact match table over the field, with each team's score, each player's stats and Pro tips against Hard bots; after a round it replaces the results table, and over the final results Tab does nothing (SB1, TL1)
- Depot: an indoor airsoft hall, 108 x 72 m under a roof with trusses and lamps, 6v6 (5v5 to 8v8 under Custom): three bent lanes round plywood rooms and racking, nine links with two dark tunnels, not mirrored but timed even, bots seeing 55 m; a BB fired up stops at the steel ceiling; positional sound and an ambience loop; BBs puff on metal, wood or concrete in its colour (FP1, DR1)
- Doors on Depot: 15 green hinged doors, some starting each round open and some shut; G (rebindable) opens or shuts the one in reach, with a prompt under the crosshair; a shut door stops BBs, bombs, sight and bodies; the creak and bang carry 20 m through walls and show on the other team's minimap; a door won't shut on someone in the doorway; bots and walk-offs open the doors in their way (DO1, MC1)
- Depot's props: its drums (teal, four to a pallet), barrels (raspberry steel, in pairs), the Lounge's sofa and shelving stocked with cartons lining every tall rack over a dark back, all the game's own Blender models recoloured off the team colours; each stands inside its old block, so cover, movement and bot routes are unchanged (DR2)
- Bots: Alpha with two bot teammates against three bots, Elimination rounds, first to five; bots see a 120 degree view, hear sound through walls muffled, take cover, peek, lean, hunt and flank, and vault low walls on their routes; the other team plays Easy, Normal or Hard (Hard pre-aims, slices corners and plays as a team) with kits from the bot kit table picked by difficulty, and your teammates play its level (BE1, BV1, BK1, DF1, DF2a, DF2b, TL1)
- Bot team play: Normal and Hard bots, friends and enemies, radio a spotted player's spot to teammates within 35 m (45 m on Hard) a moment later and come ready for them; a team two or more eliminations ahead in Elimination hunts for the rest; a team's last bot walks and checks corners; in the open, bots take a knee for each burst and sidestep between bursts; Easy keeps none of it (BQ3)
- Bot names and personalities: every bot has a name and one of four personalities (rusher, anchor, flanker, careful) that changes how it plays, never how well; names show in the Dev tab and while spectating (BN1)
- Bots by night: in the dark a bot sees you from 25 m in the open, 10 m under the trees and 15 m indoors, but from 40 m when you stand in lamplight or firelight; bots keep out of the light when they hold or hunt (WL6, TL1)
- Bots hold fire where a teammate could run into their line before the BBs arrive, a sprinting teammate round its route's corners included; a vault started against a wall's face carries on over it (WL10)
- Bot callouts: bots decide who calls what and when from `data/callouts.md` (contact, heard, down, last one, reloading, pushing, flanking, checking, holding, the flag), one call a team at a time with the browser's pace, on their own seeded stream so play never changes; your teammates' calls show in words in the hit feed with a radio click panned toward the speaker, an enemy keying up within 15 m is heard as a muffled click, and Callouts on Settings › HUD turns both off (CO1, CO2)
- Pings: the middle mouse button (rebindable, Squad: ping a spot) marks where the crosshair meets the field up to 120 m away, or an opponent you can see as a contact; one ping each at a time, a double click is one ping, cleared each round; bot teammates keep it in mind 20 s, one goes to look (or watches it when under your order or holding a post) and answers in the feed, a teammate in a fight carries on, and a teammate calling Contact may mark that enemy for you; nothing while out or on the range; your team's pings show on screen as a ring over the floor ("PING · 9 M") or a diamond where an enemy was seen ("CONTACT · 13 M"), pinned under the minimap when off screen and on the minimap, for 8 s, with a soft pip for your own; Settings › HUD › Ping markers hides the markers only (PN1, PN2)
- Bot asks: bot teammates ask for you with a callout and a marker, a HELP marker when they fight two or more enemies or defend a pole as it goes up, a GO marker to one side of an enemy they have been trading looks with, and a COVER marker on the pole as your team raises the flag; only in play and more than 15 m away, at most once every 30 s and three times a round (BQ2)
- Magazine hand-off rules: one press asks the nearest teammate within 30 m with a spare that fits for a magazine when you are low (less than one full spare), or tosses one to a teammate asking you within 6 m in sight; an ask stands 15 s, a magazine flies 0.3 s plus 0.05 s a metre and lands in the catcher's emptiest spare, or is lost if they are out or the round is decided (MH1)
- Bots share magazines: a bot low on its rifle's BBs asks the nearest teammate with one to spare ("Need a mag!"); the one asked calls "Mag coming!", sprints over, walks the last metres and tosses it once in reach and sight, never giving away its own last full spare but handing over an unused pistol's freely; fights and cover come first, then bringing one, then squad orders; a catch gets a "Thanks for the mag!" now and then; Dev › Bots run low on BBs puts one bot a team on its last magazine 8 s into each round (MH2)
- Need a Mag: H or the order wheel's Need a Mag asks a teammate for a magazine, and reads Toss a Mag while a teammate asking you is in reach; the line under the crosshair says who needs one and how far, whose is coming and what you caught, a magazine glyph marks the chevron of whoever is asking you or bringing you one, and the magazine arcs and spins from hand to chest; Settings › HUD › Mag hand-off hides the line and glyphs (MH3)
- Full-screen map: hold M for the whole field, north up, showing what the minimap shows and the area names; while it's open the mouse moves a pointer, a click sends your squad there and the ping key pings the spot under it (FM1)
- Go there: a squad order on Mouse 4, a slice on the order wheel or a click on the full-screen map; bots walk side by side to the spot and hold there looking the way they went, and with your view they stop at the first thing you look at (a wall, its foot on your side); the marker reads "Go · 30 m" on the way; with no enemy in sight and nothing heard or fought within 30 m for 30 s they give up and go back to the team plan, while Hold here never lapses (FM1, BQ1)
- Watching here: a slice on the order wheel; the spot you look at is yours to cover ("Watching · 15 m" over it, the diamond on the minimap); the nearest free teammate comes to watch it beside you and says so, at most one; the rest stay on the team plan and keep their hunts and held angles about 8 m off it; ends with another order, walking 6 m off, being hit or the round (BP8)
- Full-screen map detail: it fills the screen beside a key, under a header saying what your side must do; flags A and B (or the overtime flag's spot), each team's start in its colour, the dead zones, doors, place names sized by area that never overlap, a compass and a 10 m scale bar; the map and minimap shade the ground (grass, tracks, roads, hills, hedges and thickets) (FM2)
- Weapon torch (night only): a Light slot in Customise fits a torch to any replica; T (rebindable) switches it with a click; it goes out when you holster, are hit or a round starts; your own is a real light from Medium detail, everyone else's a visible beam, and a lit torch lets bots see its holder from 40 m; bots carry one at night and switch it by what they are doing (WL7)
- Glowing BBs: each replica in Customise picks At Night (the default), Always or Off; a glowing BB flies the same but is drawn green with a short green streak and never shrinks to nothing at range; bots load At Night (WL8)
- Bots play Attack / Defend: attackers walk to the middle then raise the flag, one on the rope and the others guarding it from cover; defenders hold near the pole, chase only what is near it, and the two nearest pull a raised flag down; two defenders on a lane stand side by side, and Hard pairs set a crossfire; with two poles attackers pick one a round and go to the pole in play, bots fighting at a rope stay on it, and defenders are sent per pole (FG2b, FG2, TL1, AB1)
- Levels and XP: matches give XP for playing, winning, each round you won and took part in, and each hit, less for a shorter match and multiplied by difficulty; levels 1 to 15 unlock replicas, power sources and parts; records keep wins and losses per difficulty and mode, best accuracy from 30 BBs and the longest win streak (LV1, SM1)
- Match numbers: every player's hits, times hit, friendly hits, BBs fired and time in play for the round and the match, and what got you on each hit (where from, how far, held angle, time in view), which the What got you card shows when your hit is called (MI1, WG1)
- Smoke and flash bombs: two grenade slots per loadout, each picked Smoke or Flash on the Loadout page (one of each by default; bots carry what their kit lists), refilled each round, taken in hand with 4 and 5 (rebindable) and thrown with a click, or held and let go; smoke hides you from bots but never BBs, and a flash blinds anyone facing it up to 22 m, teammates included, and looking away from a flash keeps you safe; bots throw theirs too; both bombs have detailed models you see in hand, in flight and on the ground (SF1, SF2, BL9)
- Bots' bombs in hand: a bot throwing a smoke or flash shows it in its right hand and throws it overhand, its arm swinging back by its head, so you see the throw (GT1)
- Your arms hold the bomb: in first person your gloved right hand holds a smoke or flash in a fist while it's in hand, each bomb with its own fitted grip (GT2); your left hand pulls the pin and your arm swings the bomb over and throws it (GT3)
- Mouse wheel: steps through your two replicas, then your smoke and flash bombs, and round again, never onto the holster; on a bomb your replica goes down, the bomb shows in your hand and a click throws it, while 1, 2, R, 3 or rolling on brings the replica back (MC1)

## Menus and settings

- Settings: a screen from the rail and the pause menu for controls, audio (Ambience starts at 20 %), display, the FPS counter and About (version, renderer, Report a Bug, Licences, Quit to desktop); every change applies and saves at once (SE1, UR1)
- HUD tab in Settings, after Display, on one screen: crosshair shape, colour, size, thickness, gap, opacity, spread and outline with a live preview; hit marker; ammo counter; hit feed corner and fade; HUD opacity, which fades only the ammo and bomb panel and minimap backgrounds; Minimap zoom from 50 % to 200 % (100 % by default), higher shows less of the field, bigger, with the two samples beside it following the slider; What got you; Callouts (HO1, CO2, MC1)
- Dev tab: cheats and tools, always there with every switch off until it is turned on: game speed, ghost, bottomless magazines, spectate bots, retro filter (a 1990s console look, with Lines and Shades sliders), reset save (DV2), disable levelling and reset level (LV1, FB3)
- Match screen: pick a map from picture cards (Depot or Woodland; Woodland is an open wood of 176 x 120 m on gentle slopes, a shallow stream down the middle that slows wading and splashes loudly (WS1), three lanes, huts you walk into, fern beds that hide a crouched player and bushes a standing one, WR1), the mode (Attack / Defend picked first where the map has a flagpole), your team (ALPHA or BETA) and a Bots section with two rows, Opponents and Teammates, each Easy, Normal or Hard (Teammates follow the opponents until picked), with a Rules section under the modes: Skirmish (rules as chips) or Custom (five switches: team size, from the picked map's own sizes: 5v5 to 8v8 on every field, 6v6 standard (TS1), rounds to win, round time, friendly fire, ricochets, each saved as you change it), and a Day / Night row for a map that offers both (Woodland, day first, WR1; Depot plays by day; Settings › Dev › Time of day forces either); picks stay while the game is open (MN2, MN3, WL3, WL4, WL5)
- Practice on any field: pick Practice on the Match screen for no bots and no score, start at Alpha's first spawn with targets worked out from the map (lane points, the other team's spawns, along the lanes, steel, movers and swingers) and a timed challenge whose START stands ahead of you and lights only targets in sight; each map keeps its own best. The practice range stands in Depot's site with Depot's skyline, its own layout unchanged (WL9); its steel plates, flat plywood figure cut-outs, distance boards, fence walls, sandbag walls, site toilet, water tote and generator are modelled from photos (RM1)
- Loading screen: PLAY shows the map's picture, name and a progress bar; once the match has fully loaded it says Ready and shows a CONTINUE button (click, Enter or Space) that starts the round clock and the ambience (nothing plays while it loads); coming back from the Loadout goes straight in (MN2, MC1)
- Pause menu: PAUSED, the map, mode and round, RESUME, SETTINGS, Skip to next round while you can skip, QUIT MATCH and the match's seed (MN2, WG1)
- Menus: charcoal panels with off-white words over a blurred view of the field you picked, one pale bone accent and condensed capitals for headings; a slim text rail down the left with AIRSOFT, MATCH, LOADOUT, ARMORY, SETTINGS, your level with a thin XP bar and QUIT. No title screen: the game opens on Match with PLAY ready (MN1, UR1)
- Loadout and Customise screens: the LOADOUT tab with five saved loadouts, your carried replicas and a Performance sheet; CUSTOMISE sets each replica's colour, BB weight, hop-up and power source (LO2)
- Locked items: a replica, power source or part above your level is listed greyed with "LEVEL n" and can't be picked; the rail's level line shows yours, with a thin XP bar (LV1)
- Button feedback: every menu and pause button dips and flashes an accent outline on click or Enter (outline only with Reduced motion); the rail's level greys, not hides, when levelling is disabled (UB1)
- Armory and end-of-match XP: the ARMORY tab with your level, XP, unlocks and a level track of ten levels per page with arrows to switch pages, and the XP and level-up card in the middle of the match summary (AR1, SM1)
- Match summary: after the last round's table, a WON or LOST stamp with the score, map and bots; everyone's match numbers, the XP card, your records with NEW RECORD tags, What got you for your latest five hits and the next Pro tip; the next match starts after 15 s or on a click (SM1)
