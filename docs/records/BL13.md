## What changed

- **Pads** (`tools/blender/figures/pads.py`): the human figure's knee and elbow pads, the `Kit_Pads` part of
  `human.glb` (LOD0 2,964, LOD1 976, LOD2 288 triangles, all four pads; budget 3,000 / 1,000 / 300 agreed with the body
  rebuild, BL10). Traced from the owner's second knee pad photo (`ref_images.zip` › `clothes/knee_pads/81uLdVEli5L`):
  a cordura cushion with a piped edge and a stitch line; the hourglass shell with a raised tongue, two slot vents, a
  raised knee dome and six eyelet rivets; two elastic straps round the back of the limb, each hooked by a triangle
  buckle over a stud on a tab at the outer edge. The pads are fitted by a radius field cast from each limb's axis onto
  the `Body` in its rest pose (smoothed and never inside it), so a rerun on a rebuilt body refits them. Detail is a
  1024 colour, normal and roughness-metal set painted in the photo's own pixels (the UVs are the design's
  coordinates); one material `Pads`.
- **Fit to the photo:** heights off the pad's face stand off mostly forward (LIFT_TURN), so the shell sits inside
  the cushion as in the photo instead of spreading past it; the pad is cupped round the joint (DOME_RISE). The webbing
  is one face; each buckle hooks over a modelled stud on the cushion's side edge.
- **Weights:** each pad's shell rides its shin or forearm rigidly, and carries its buckles and studs; only the knee
  cushion's soft top band, above the shell, eases to 20 % thigh (PAD_BEND over BEND_BAND), so crouched it folds onto
  the thigh. The straps take the body's own weights where they lie on it (Blender's data transfer), blended in past the
  buckle's far bar, so they stay on the sleeve and trouser leg when the joint bends and turn with the forearm's twist.
  They follow a lightly smoothed copy of the radius field, so they lie on the cloth rather than bridging its folds as
  the pad does.
- **Splicing** (`tools/blender/figures/splice_part.py`, plain Python): copies the `Kit_Pads*` nodes into `human.glb`
  with their mesh, material and textures, the joints mapped to the figure's skin by bone name after checking both
  rest poses match; the rest of the file is kept. The body rebuild (BL10) uses the same tool for its `Body`.
- **Game:** no code change: the sidecar lists the three parts, so `FigureBody` draws them at their levels and the
  LOD2 joins the shadow twin. `tests/unit/test_figure_pads.gd` checks the parts, budgets, bones and material;
  `test_figure_body.gd` and `test_figure_models.gd` take the new counts.

## Decisions

- No elbow photo: the elbow pads are the knee design at 0.72 scale (the usual matching set), with fewer outline
  points (0.78) to stay in budget.
- Of the three photos, two show the same X-shell pad with slot vents; that design is modelled (the honeycomb-vent pad
  of the first photo is not). Colour black (two of three photos). Unbranded.
- Robots wear no pads (bare robot).
- Each level uses about 95 % of its budget, the owner's rule for every model (2026-11-05).
- Crouched, the rigid knee shell's top stands off the thigh and its upper strap runs from the buckle down round the
  thigh. A real pad hinges half way about the knee; the 65-bone rig is fixed (no knee helper bone) and blended
  weights cannot turn a rigid part half way without bending or shrinking it, so the shell stays rigid on the shin.
- The CrouchIdle clip rests a hand on the bare knee, but in game the aim clip drives the arms (animation thread), so
  crouch renders use CrouchIdle legs with the Rifle_Aim upper body.
- v12's own knee pads were part of its `Body`; the rebuilt body (BL10, #212) cut them out as an interim `Kit_Pads`, which
  these pads replace (spliced over the merged body, 2026-11-05; the KNOWN_ISSUES row is closed).
