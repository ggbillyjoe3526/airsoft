# Parts and Power

Parts and power sources change how a replica plays, never how hard a hit lands. Fit them on the **Customise** screen
of your [Loadout](Loadout); each part shows on the replica in your hands and in the menu pictures. Locked parts stay
listed, greyed out with the level that unlocks them ([Levels and Unlocks](Levels-and-Unlocks)).

The tables are read from the game's own files. How to read them:

- **×** multiplies a time or an amount: below 1 is quicker or smaller, above 1 slower or bigger.
- **%** adds that share on top: +10 % is a tenth more.
- **–** means no change.
- **Handling** is the time to bring the replica up after a switch and to raise a fitted optic.

A replica with no parts plays exactly as it comes, and bots on the other team carry parts by their
[kit](Bots#kits).

## Power sources

What drives the replica. A **battery** sets an electric replica's rate of fire: the LiPo fires faster and kicks a
third harder, so a held trigger climbs off a far target sooner. **Gas** sets a gas replica's energy, and a stronger
gas kicks harder too: Green Gas is the one for quick follow-up shots, Red Gas reaches a little further, and Black Gas
reaches furthest, kicks hardest and cycles the slide a little slower. A **spring** sets the Sniper Rifle's energy, how quickly you
can work the bolt, how hard it kicks and how far the shot is heard. The M130 is the quick, quiet one: the fastest
bolt, heard from less far. The M140 is the middle one: more reach for a slightly slower bolt and more kick. The M150
is the long shot: the most reach, paid for with the slowest bolt, a kick that jumps the view through a scope and a
crack heard from further. **Heard from** is how much further the shot is heard, by bots, on the minimap and on the
sound cues. Energy still stops at the [site limit](Replicas#site-limits).
Gas shoots softer on a cold field ([How to Play](How-to-Play#wind-and-weather)), so a stronger gas earns its place
on a cold night.

The menus show each one as it looks at the field: the **Standard Battery** a red NiMH brick pack, the **11.1 V LiPo**
a black stick pack with its balance lead, the gases one 1 litre can whose cap and shoulder are the gas's colour, and
the springs a painted rifle spring in each spring's colour.

## Optics

An optic on the top rail. With one fitted the iron sights come off completely, so nothing stands in its way, and
aiming looks through it. Every optic is weight on the rail, so the replica is a little slower to bring up after a
switch than on its iron sights: no sight is free. The red dot is a compact sight on a low riser: a lit dot on clear glass, with the view open
round it, and the quickest sight to raise. The 2x scope is a magnifier on a low hinged mount, the quicker of the two
scopes to raise; it magnifies only inside its glass: the view round it
stays as it is, with nothing dark round the edge. At the hip a scope's glass is clear glass showing what the scope points at (on Low graphics, tinted glass); the
magnified view and its reticle show only while you aim. The Variable Scope is a plain compact prism scope, one small
dial on top, with a zoom: aimed
through it, the mouse wheel (Zoom in and Zoom out in Settings › Controls) turns its zoom from 2x to 6x a step at a
time (the HUD shows it), and it keeps that zoom through the match. It is the slowest sight to raise.

<!-- wiki:parts optics -->

- **Zoom** is how much aiming narrows the view. **Raise time** is the time to bring it to your eye. **Draw** is the
  time to bring the replica up with it fitted.

## Grips

A grip under the rail. **Shake** is how long the wobble of a sprint or a landing stays in your aim. **Moving
spread** is the share that is left of the extra spread walking, running and jumping add.

<!-- wiki:parts grips -->

A vertical grip is the control pick: steadier while you move and sooner steady after a sprint, but slower to bring
up. An angled grip is the speed pick: quicker to bring up and to raise a sight, but the wobble of a sprint stays
longer. Your left
hand holds the grip you fit: it wraps a vertical grip, and with an angled one cups the handguard with the palm
against its swept face.

## Lasers

<!-- wiki:parts lasers -->

The laser is a closed unit under the GBB Pistol's rail, or on the left side rail of the AEG Rifle's handguard, just
behind your left hand, its emitter flush in the front. It is the part for shooting from the hip: with the sight down
your shots group a quarter tighter (**Hip spread**), most useful on the move; with the sight raised to your eye it
changes nothing, and the unit on the rail makes the replica a touch slower to bring up. The AEG's torch takes the
right side rail, so the two fit together.

## Magazines

Each replica's standard magazine is how it comes; these are the alternatives. **Capacity** multiplies the BBs a
magazine holds and **Carried** adds magazines to the replica's own (you always carry at least one).

<!-- wiki:parts magazines -->

- **Looks:** each is its replica's own magazine at another length. The hi-cap is a third longer, a pressed steel
  shell with narrow ribs, a welded seam down its back and a winding wheel under its base; the low-cap is short,
  moulded polymer with wide ribs; the pistol's extended magazine sticks out below the grip, holes down its back.
- **Rattles:** the loose BBs in a hi-cap rattle as you move, so bots close by hear you even walking, and your run
  and sprint carry 7 m further.
- The hi-cap carries as many BBs in all in fewer, rattling changes, each one slower; the low-cap carries fewer BBs
  in all for the quickest change and draw. The pistol's extended magazine holds half as many again, for a slower draw.

## Barrels

<!-- wiki:parts barrels -->

The tight bore is the accuracy pick: tighter groups at no handling cost, for a little less energy and reach. The
long barrel is the reach pick: the most energy, so the flattest and quickest shot, but no tighter, and front-heavy. Both count toward the site limit. Each goes inside the rifle: the tight
bore's polished crown just shows in the flash hider, and the long barrel adds a 10 cm barrel extension that carries
the flash hider or silencer out to its end.

## Muzzle parts

<!-- wiki:parts muzzle -->

**Heard from** is how far away its shots are heard, by bots, on the minimap and on the sound cues: ×0.5 is half as
far. Silenced shots also sound muffled and quieter.

## Lights

<!-- wiki:parts lights -->

A **weapon torch** fits any replica in the Light slot. It is fitted only on a field played at night (by day it does
nothing and isn't there), and **T** switches it on and off (rebind it in Settings › Controls). It goes out when you
holster, switch to a replica without one, are hit, and at the start of each round. **Reach** is how far its beam lights
someone well enough to be made out, by you and by bots; **Beam** is the bright hotspot's angle and **Spill** the dimmer
light round it. A torch lights the way and gives you away: bots see anyone standing in a lit beam, or pointing a lit
torch near them, from as far as its reach. Every bot carries one at night and switches it on in a close fight and at
the end of a search. On Medium graphics and up your torch is a real light from the rail, with shadows on High and
Ultra; on Low it is a soft haze. On the GBB Pistol, whose one rail the torch shares with the laser, the torch hangs
under the laser when both are fitted.
